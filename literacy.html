What is Literacy?

The Foundation of All Learning

Literacy is the ability to read, write, listen, and speak in ways that allow children to understand and engage with the world around them. At Jets Elementary, we treat literacy not as a single subject but as the bedrock of every other area of learning.

Aligned with Kenya's Competency-Based Curriculum (CBC), our literacy programme develops confident communicators from Pre-Primary through Grade 4 — ensuring every child finds their voice and falls in love with language.

How We Teach

Our Teaching Approach

Our literacy instruction is learner-centred, joyful, and aligned with the CBC framework. We believe children learn to read and write best when they are engaged, supported, and celebrated.

Interactive Activities Hands-on learning through games, role-play, and group discussions that make language come alive.
Creative Expression Storytelling, drawing, poetry, and creative writing that build love for language and personal voice.
Collaborative Learning Peer reading, shared writing, and group discussions that build communication and confidence.
Differentiated Instruction Lessons tailored to each child's pace and level so no learner is left behind or held back.
FAQs

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions parents ask about our literacy programme.

At what age should my child start learning to read?

We introduce pre-reading skills in PP1 (age 4) with letter recognition and phonics. Most children begin reading simple words by age 5–6. Every child develops at their own pace, and our teachers provide individualised support throughout the process.

How do you support children who struggle with reading?

We use differentiated instruction, small group sessions, one-on-one support, and multisensory approaches. Our teachers are trained in reading intervention strategies and work closely with parents to track and celebrate each child's progress.

What reading materials do you use in class?

We use a rich variety of age-appropriate materials — phonics readers, picture storybooks, CBC-aligned textbooks, digital resources, and our school library with books from diverse Kenyan and global authors. Every child has access to reading materials at their level.

How can parents support literacy learning at home?

We recommend reading together daily (even 10 minutes helps), creating a quiet reading corner at home, visiting the library regularly, and playing word games. We send home monthly reading tips and recommended book lists to help parents stay engaged.
